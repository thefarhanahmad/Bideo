Starting a video channel feels big, but the first steps are simple. You don't need a studio or expensive gear — just a phone, an idea and a plan you can stick to. Here's how beginners in India can start strong.

## 1. Pick a niche you can sustain

A niche is the main topic of your channel. Good beginner niches are ones you **already enjoy** and can make **many videos** about:

- Comedy skits and relatable situations
- Cooking and street-food recipes
- Study tips, exam preparation and general knowledge
- Fitness, yoga and home workouts
- Devotional and festival content
- Regional music, dance and shayari
- Tech tips, phone tricks and app tutorials

> **Tip:** Write down 20 video ideas for your niche. If that's easy, it's a good niche. See [100+ short video ideas](/blog/short-video-ideas-for-indian-creators) for inspiration.

## 2. The only equipment you really need

- **A smartphone** with a decent camera.
- **Light** — face a window during the day, or use one affordable ring light.
- **Clear audio** — record in a quiet room; a basic clip-on mic helps a lot.
- **A simple tripod or phone stand** for steady shots.

More details in [how to shoot short videos on your phone](/blog/how-to-shoot-short-videos-on-phone).

## 3. Plan before you press record

- **Hook (first 2 seconds):** a question, a surprising visual or a bold statement.
- **Value (middle):** the joke, the recipe step, the tip.
- **Ending:** a clear finish and a reason to follow for more.

## 4. Set up your Bideo channel

Create your account, choose a memorable username and fill in your profile photo, channel name and bio. Follow the [account setup guide](/blog/how-to-create-bideo-account).

## 5. Publish your first 10 videos

Don't wait for perfect. Publish 10 videos, then look at which ones got the most views and comments, and make more like those. Use clear titles and relevant hashtags — see the [titles and hashtags guide](/blog/short-video-titles-and-hashtags-guide).

## 6. Be consistent and patient

Growth usually comes from steady posting over weeks, not one lucky video. Pick a schedule you can keep — for example **3 to 5 shorts per week** — and stick to it.

## 7. Engage with your audience

Reply to comments, pin a good comment, and post a community update once in a while. People follow creators who feel real and responsive.

## Common beginner mistakes

- Copying other creators' videos or using content you don't own (this can lead to removal).
- Long, slow intros — viewers swipe away within seconds.
- Changing topics every day so nobody knows what your channel is about.
- Giving up after a few videos.
